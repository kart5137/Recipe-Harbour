# Model card: Recipe Harbour photo recogniser

> Generated by `model/clip_model_card.py` on 2026-11-23 from the saved results in `model/runs/clip/`. Do not edit by hand.

## What it does

Recognises **one fruit, vegetable or herb per photo** and suggests ingredients from the Recipe Harbour vocabulary, with a probability. It runs in the browser (ONNX Runtime Web); photos never leave the device. It is a convenience for adding ingredients, not a food-safety tool.

It replaces the R6 model (MobileNetV2, 24 classes, 10 offered), which did not know onion or garlic.

## How it works

- An image-text model, **Google SigLIP 2 base, patch 32, 256 × 256** (`google/siglip2-base-patch32-256`, Apache 2.0), turns the photo into a vector.
- Each of the 74 ingredients has a few names ("radish" also covers turnip and parsnip). Their text vectors from the same model are the starting point of the ingredient head; the head was then tuned on real photos, with a penalty that keeps it close to the text (strength 0.3, 30 passes, chosen on held-out training photos). Ingredients without training photos still work from their names.
- An ingredient's score is its best-matching name; a softmax turns scores into the percentages the app shows.
- Red onion and shallot are answered as **onion**: the model cannot tell them apart reliably.
- Only the image half of the model ships. Its matrix weights are stored in 4 bits (blocks of 32), other large weights in 16 bits: **66.5 MB**. The ingredient head is 232 kB.

## How well it works

Across all 74 ingredients (the R6 model chose among 24).

**Shipped model (q4h):** right first time 76 % / in the top 3 91 % on the 2,205 test crops; 78 % / 91 % on the 1,142 whole photos. (R6 model: 74 % / 90 % on the crops, over 24 classes.)

When the app says "Looks like X" (probability at least 60 % and X offered), X is right on 94 % of those test photos (1,985 answers); per ingredient in the table below.

TinyCLIP would be about half the size but is clearly less accurate. (Patch 16 was not trained with a head.)

## Known weaknesses

- Measured but below the bar: coriander (30 % right first), zucchini (49 % right first), cucumber (53 % right first), cauliflower (56 % right first), mango (57 % right first), coconut (58 % right first), chili pepper (63 % right first), raspberry (76 % right first), green onion (77 % right first), peas (77 % right first), blueberry (90 % right first).
- Cucumber and zucchini look alike; berries are often confused with each other.
- Not trained for several items in one photo, cut or cooked food, or packaged food (use the barcode scanner).
- About 0.7 s per photo on a desktop browser (one WebAssembly thread); slower on phones.
